love, lust, and longing

March 21, 2026

I have spent the better part of this month on cloud nine, which, if you've never been, is a terrible place to live. The rent is free but the eviction notice always comes.

Ten days. That's all it takes to love or hate someone completely when traveling with them. There is no middle ground, no scenic route, no extended trial period. We were in New York together, then met back up for Aspen, and then he escorted me back to DC.

But here's what I keep circling back to: is it him, or is it the sex?

For nearly three years I was celibate, which is a word that sounds a lot more spiritual than it felt. It was less a calling and more a prolonged hostage situation I'd arranged for myself. I was terrified of disappointment, so I simply opted out. The longer I waited, the more impossible the return became. I also genuinely enjoy being alone. Most people bore me. They are predictable in their tastes and unimaginative in their suffering, and I long ago stopped auditioning explanations for who I am to audiences who don't deserve the show.

Then I met him.

He is handsome the way certain men are handsome. Not aggressively, not accidentally, but with the quiet ease of someone who has always been looked at and made peace with it. Think: the man outside the club in Barcelona at 3am, smoking a cigarette the night before your flight home, wearing something that costs more than your rent and somehow making you feel underdressed in your own skin. 6'2. Cyclist. Cross country skier. Dresses like he wandered off a Brunello Cucinelli runway and forgot to go back. I met him in a New York City restaurant and sent over a cocktail with a napkin, on which I jotted down my number and a brief demand letter to ask me out. No active social media, no dating apps. He follows ONE account on Instagram and that is the company he founded. No list of mids he collected over the years from dating apps. I'm sorry, but that is peak sexy. This man's existence lit up my girls group chat and rekindled the romanticization of a good 'ol fashion in-person meetcute. Best $35 ever spent.

But none of that is the point.

The point is how he talks about his life. He skippered boats in his twenties before co-founding a startup that became a unicorn, and he did all of this without ever becoming the kind of person you'd want to avoid at a dinner party. He never climbed to a new social circle. He just pulled his old one up with him. In ten days of conversation I never once heard him take anything for granted or speak badly of anyone. Not once. Do you know how rare that is?

He is so relentlessly helpful that he crouched down on the floor of a fine dining restaurant to help a server pick up a dropped plate, then tipped him 100% of our $600 bill when we left. He makes you aware, in his presence, of every time you haven't done the equivalent thing. Across a hotel room he was silently mouthing at me to push our dinner reservation back fifteen minutes so he could not only advise a nineteen-year-old German startup founder he's angel investing, but actually help rebuild some API script thing. This is where he lost me entirely. I smiled and nodded and understood nothing, which it turns out is its own kind of intimacy.

The sex is extraordinary. Eight times a day. Not counting shower sex. The full catastrophe. I had been repressed for so long I forgot this was available to me as an option. I am free now. FREEEEE. The shackles are off and I intend to be absolutely insufferable about it.

Except.

A month in, I am in a relationship again. He asked me to be his girlfriend. He asked me to move to Germany. I actually seriously considered it for about 48 hours. I introduced him to my mom. We facetimed his parents.

The last time I followed a German tech bro to a new city it did not go well. I will leave it at that.

Things are different now anyway. I have a business to finish building, a postbacc in SF with my best friend, vague but sincere plans for a bakery, a son I want to raise somewhere warm and good surrounded by other smart wasian kids. I need to stay somewhere long enough for something to take root. I have spent years shedding lives like coats at the end of a season, leaving versions of myself in cities that have since moved on without me. I don't want to do that anymore. DC is no longer my home, but it's hard to abandon a city where your work lives. I will just have to build somewhere else, brick by brick, like a sensible person.

For now I am going to drown in this romance and do unspeakable things and enjoy every second of it. Next week I am going to ghost him and grieve my own self-inflicted heartbreak.

I simply cannot fathom someone this perfect deciding to leave me.

So I will have to leave first.
